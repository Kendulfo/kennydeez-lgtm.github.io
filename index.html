AIA Digital Design System

Building a comprehensive design system from scratch for a fast-growing insurance business

Role
UX Consultant
Duration
2 Years
Team
3 designers, 4 developers, 1 PM
Impact
60% faster dev time

Overview

When I joined AIA, they had grown from 5 to 50 designers in 18 months. Their product had evolved organically, resulting in inconsistent UI patterns, duplicated components, and a development process that was slowing down with each new feature. I was tasked with creating a comprehensive design system that would scale with the company's rapid growth.

The Challenge

The main challenge was creating a system that could adapt to different regions while being flexible enough to accommodate rapid feature development. We needed to establish design principles, create a token system, build 50+ components, and migrate existing products without disrupting ongoing development.

The Solution

I led the creation of a comprehensive design system built on atomic design principles. We established a robust token system, created extensive component documentation, and implemented a phased migration strategy that allowed teams to adopt the system gradually.

Design Process

1

Discovery & Audit

  • Conducted a comprehensive audit of existing UI patterns across all products, identifying 200+ unique variations of what should have been standard components.
  • Interviewed stakeholders from design, engineering, and product teams to understand pain points and requirements.
  • Analyzed competitor design systems and industry best practices to inform our approach.
2

Foundation & Principles

  • Established core design principles focused on clarity, efficiency, and scalability that would guide all design decisions.
  • Created a comprehensive token system covering colors, typography, spacing, and motion that could adapt to light and dark themes.
  • Defined component hierarchy and naming conventions that would scale with the system's growth.
3

Component Development

  • Built 50+ components starting with foundational elements like buttons and inputs, then progressing to complex patterns.
  • Each component included multiple variants, states, and comprehensive documentation with usage guidelines.
  • Collaborated closely with engineers to ensure components were built with accessibility, performance, and developer experience in mind.
4

Documentation & Testing

  • Created extensive documentation including design guidelines, component specs, and implementation examples.
  • Set up automated visual regression testing to catch breaking changes early in the development process.
  • Established governance processes for proposing, reviewing, and implementing changes to the system.

Key Learnings

  • Early collaboration with engineering teams is crucial for creating components that are both design-forward and developer-friendly.
  • Incremental adoption works better than big-bang migrations — teams can adopt the system at their own pace while seeing immediate benefits.
  • Documentation is just as important as the components themselves — clear guidelines prevent misuse and ensure consistency.
  • Building a governance process early helps maintain system quality as it scales and more contributors join.
  • Regular audit cycles help identify gaps and opportunities for improvement as the product evolves.
  • Token systems provide flexibility for theming and ensure visual consistency across all components and products.

Mobile Banking App

Redesigning a mobile banking interface with focus on accessibility and user experience

Role
Senior Product Designer
Duration
6 months
Team
2 designers, 3 developers, 1 PM
Impact
2.1 → 4.6 star rating

Overview

The existing mobile banking app had low user satisfaction scores and poor accessibility compliance. Users struggled with complex navigation and critical banking tasks. I led a complete redesign focusing on simplifying user flows and ensuring the app met WCAG 2.1 AA standards.

The Challenge

The existing app had a 2.1 star rating in app stores, with users citing confusing navigation, slow transaction flows, and poor accessibility. We needed to redesign the entire experience while maintaining security standards and regulatory compliance.

The Solution

We conducted extensive user research to understand pain points, redesigned the information architecture, and created a mobile-first design system. The new design prioritized clarity, speed, and accessibility across all user flows.

Design Process

1

User Research

  • Conducted usability testing with 20 existing users to identify key pain points and friction areas.
  • Performed accessibility audits and tested with users who rely on assistive technologies.
  • Analyzed support tickets and app store reviews to understand common issues and user needs.
2

Information Architecture

  • Redesigned the app's navigation structure based on user mental models and task frequency.
  • Simplified the flow for core banking tasks like transfers, bill pay, and account management.
  • Created a dashboard that surfaces the most relevant information and actions for each user.
3

Visual Design & Prototyping

  • Applied our design system to create a cohesive mobile experience with larger touch targets and improved contrast ratios.
  • Built high-fidelity prototypes to test new interaction patterns and validate design decisions.
  • Ensured all designs met WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility standards from the start.
4

Testing & Iteration

  • Conducted multiple rounds of usability testing throughout the design process.
  • Worked with the QA team to ensure accessibility compliance and smooth implementation.
  • Created detailed specifications and worked closely with developers during implementation.

Results & Impact

Key Learnings

  • Accessibility considerations should be integrated from the beginning of the design process, not added as an afterthought.
  • Mobile banking users prioritize speed and clarity over visual complexity — simple interfaces perform better.
  • Regular testing with real users throughout the design process catches issues that internal teams miss.
  • Working closely with developers during implementation ensures the final product matches the design vision.
  • Security and compliance requirements can actually improve UX when properly considered in the design process.
